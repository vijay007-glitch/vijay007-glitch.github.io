Light Painter

Paint a photomask on a 64 × 64 grid and watch the resist contour print through a partially coherent EUV or ArF imaging model. Six puzzles in optical proximity correction, plus a sandbox with a Bossung plot and depth-of-focus readout.

October 5, 2026, interactive simulator

A projection lens is a low-pass filter. It cannot pass the fine spatial frequencies that a sharp corner or a short line end needs, so a mask drawn exactly like the design prints with rounded corners and pulled-back line ends. Optical proximity correction (OPC) is the inverse problem: draw the mask so that the printed shape comes out right. In this lab you do it by hand, one cell at a time, and the resist contour answers immediately.

The imaging model is Abbe partially coherent imaging on a 128 × 128 periodic FFT grid, followed by a threshold resist that prints where dose × intensity exceeds 30% of the clear-field intensity. Defocus enters as a pupil phase, so the illumination shape, σ and focus behave the way they do in a scanner. The model is scalar and thin-mask: it has no polarization, 3D mask effects, flare or stochastics. It is a companion to the High-NA EUV post, which covers the Rayleigh equations behind the optics controls.

Things to try:

  • Start on level 1. The 64 nm square prints with a worst edge error of 39.2% of the CD. Pick the 3-cell brush and click the cell just diagonally outside each corner. The error drops to 7.9% and earns three stars.
  • Stars come from the worst edge-placement gauge: one star at 25% of the CD or better, two at 15%, three at 10%. Any bridge, break, missing feature or stray print scores zero.
  • On level 4 the two tips face each other across a one-CD gap. Extending the tips closes the gap and bridges them. A one-cell bar along each side of each tip pushes them back without touching.
  • Level 5 switches to annular light with the dose set for the dense lines, so the lone line prints thin. Assist bars beside it peak at an intensity of 0.294 against a print threshold of 0.310, close to printing but not over it.
  • Levels 3, 4 and 6 have a par of two stars. A third star would need single-cell specks that a mask shop's mask-rule check would reject, so the game does not offer that route.
  • Open Show solution on any level to see the reference mask, step through its edits, and paint it onto the reticle. Auto-OPC does a greedy cleanup for you. Stars are not recorded while either is in use, and your own stars are saved only in your browser.
  • Change the scanner, NA, CD, illumination or σ and scoring pauses, so High-NA cannot trivially win a level. Switching scanner or NA keeps the physical CD and moves k1, which is limited to 0.10 to 1.00.
  • Switch to Sandbox for dense lines, a contact array, a logic snippet or the letters LP. Set the mask as the target, pick a CD site, and read the Bossung plot, which shows CD through focus at ±5% dose, and its depth of focus.

This is an educational model, not a production OPC tool. The equations, constants and simplifications are in the "How the model works" panel at the bottom of the lab.
