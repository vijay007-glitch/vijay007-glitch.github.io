Multi-Vt Cell Explorer

Sweep temperature, supply, process corner, body bias and gate length across ULVT, LVT, SVT and HVT transistors and the SRAM bitcell built from them, and watch threshold, leakage, delay and stability trade against each other.

October 3, 2026, interactive simulator

Standard-cell libraries ship the same logic in several threshold-voltage flavors so that timing-critical paths can use fast, leaky cells while everything else uses slow, frugal ones. This lab puts NMOS and PMOS transistors in four flavors (ULVT, LVT, SVT and HVT) side by side for three technology classes: planar bulk 28 nm, FinFET 16 nm and FD-SOI 22 nm. A third tab builds a 6T SRAM bitcell from the same devices.

Open the simulator full screen. On a phone, that works better than the embedded view below.

Things to try:

  • Lower VDD below the ZTC supply shown in the table, then sweep temperature. Cold becomes the slow corner: temperature inversion. On the PMOS tab, the HVT pull-up is already there at nominal 0.80 V.
  • Open the leakage components for HVT and set −40 °C. Gate tunneling barely changes with temperature, so it sets a floor that a higher Vth cannot remove.
  • Pick the SF or FS corner and compare the NMOS fall delay with the PMOS rise delay: the skewed corners pull them apart.
  • In planar 28 nm, push reverse body bias past about −0.3 V. Junction leakage climbs and total leakage rises again.
  • In the 6T SRAM tab, set the FS corner and 125 °C. Read stability drops and the array misses its yield target. Then try SF at −40 °C, where writes are hardest.
  • Lower the word line to 90%. Read yield improves, and the bitline has to go lower to write the cell.
  • In the block budget, note that the 4% of logic cells that are ULVT produce 28% of the block's standby power, more than the half of the cells that are SVT. The SRAM array is about a third of the total.

The device model is a compact physics model with representative parameters, not foundry PDK data. The equations and parameter values are in the "Model and assumptions" section at the bottom of the lab.
