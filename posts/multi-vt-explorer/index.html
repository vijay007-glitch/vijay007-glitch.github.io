Multi-Vt Cell Explorer

Sweep temperature, supply, process corner, body bias and gate length across ULVT, LVT, SVT and HVT devices, and watch threshold voltage, leakage and delay trade against each other.

October 3, 2026, interactive simulator

Standard-cell libraries ship the same logic in several threshold-voltage flavors so that timing-critical paths can use fast, leaky cells while everything else uses slow, frugal ones. This lab puts one NMOS transistor in four flavors (ULVT, LVT, SVT and HVT) side by side for three technology classes: planar bulk 28 nm, FinFET 16 nm and FD-SOI 22 nm.

Open the simulator full screen. On a phone, that works better than the embedded view below.

Things to try:

  • Lower VDD below the ZTC supply shown in the table, then sweep temperature. Cold becomes the slow corner: temperature inversion.
  • Open the leakage components for HVT and set −40 °C. Gate tunneling barely changes with temperature, so it sets a floor that a higher Vth cannot remove.
  • In planar 28 nm, push reverse body bias past about −0.3 V. Junction leakage climbs and total leakage rises again.
  • In FD-SOI 22 nm, sweep back-gate bias across ±1.8 V and compare how much more it moves Vth than in the other two technologies.
  • In the Vt-mix budget, note that 4% ULVT cells produce about 41% of the block's leakage at the default corner.

The device model is a compact physics model with representative parameters, not foundry PDK data. The equations and parameter values are in the "Model and assumptions" section at the bottom of the lab.
