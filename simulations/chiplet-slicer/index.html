How the model works

Dies per wafer
Whole dies on a grid centred on a 300 mm wafer, with 3 mm edge exclusion and 0.1 mm scribe lanes. Partial dies at the edge are lost. The textbook estimate πr²/S − πd/√(2S) gives for the current die; the exact count is .
Yield
Poisson, Y = e−A·D₀, with A in cm² including PHY area. The map scatters real random defects at density D₀, so each new wafer lands near the Poisson value. Poisson assumes defects are independent; real defects cluster, which negative-binomial models capture and which lets very large dies yield somewhat better than shown here.
Splitting
The die is cut into equal chiplets on the same node and wafer. Each die-to-die link puts a PHY block on both chiplets it joins, so a 2 × 2 split has 4 links and 8 PHY blocks. Chiplets are tested before assembly (known-good die), so only good ones are packaged. Test cost is not modelled.
Packaging
Assembly yield is the per-chiplet yield raised to the number of chiplets, and a failed assembly scraps every good chiplet in it. Bridges add a cost per link; an interposer adds cost per mm² of silicon it carries, with 10% margin.
Reticle limit
A scanner exposes at most 26 × 33 mm (858 mm²) at once. A monolithic die beyond that needs stitching across fields, so its column is hatched and its cost is hypothetical. The same check applies to each chiplet.
Signatures and triggers
Edge rings, scratches, particle clusters and reticle repeaters are added on top of the random defects. They change this wafer's measured yield and the analysis, but the cost chart stays on the Poisson plan. A wafer is flagged for root cause analysis when its good-die count falls more than 3σ (binomial) below the Poisson baseline, or when a spatial check finds structure: a low radial zone, a low reticle position, or fails that touch each other.
