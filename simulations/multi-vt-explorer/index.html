Model and assumptions

Each flavor is the same transistor with a different threshold set by work function (FinFET, FD-SOI) or channel doping (planar). Parameters are representative values chosen to reproduce published trends for each technology class. They are not foundry PDK data. PMOS uses its own parameters (lower hole mobility, its own threshold and temperature coefficients) and is described with magnitudes: |VGS|, |VDS|, |ID|.

Vth,sat is extracted at ID = 100 nA × W/L with full VDD on the drain. The inverter's PMOS is sized so that rise and fall delays match for SVT at TT, 25 °C and nominal VDD, and that delay is scaled to a typical FO4 value for the node. Skewed corners (SF, FS) then split rise and fall. The ZTC supply is where delay at −40 °C equals delay at 125 °C; below it, cold is the slow corner. Vfwd is the body-bias slider: forward bias is positive for both devices, so NMOS VBS = Vfwd and PMOS VBS = −Vfwd. In FD-SOI the back gate sits under the buried oxide, so back bias does not stress any junction.

6T SRAM bitcell

The butterfly curves are the DC transfer curves of the two cross-coupled inverters, solved node by node with the transistor model above. In read mode the access transistors connect both nodes to bitlines held at VDD, with the word line at the chosen level. The static noise margin (SNM) is the side of the largest square that fits inside each lobe, found by rotating the axes 45° and taking the largest vertical gap between the curves (Seevinck's method); the cell's SNM is the smaller lobe.

The Monte Carlo draws the same 200 sets of random numbers every time, scaled by each transistor's σVth, so the histogram moves smoothly as you change conditions. μ/σ from 200 samples carries roughly ±5% sampling error. The write trip uses the standard pull-up versus access-transistor approximation; a full transient write would flip slightly earlier.
